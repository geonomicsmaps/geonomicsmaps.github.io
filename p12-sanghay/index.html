  • CCTV News (June 7, 2025; republished by Huanqiu), 2025

Our calculations

  • "About sixty-nine million dollars": compensation of 476.96 million yuan (bond prospectus, 2021) at the 2019 average rate of 6.9081 yuan per dollar (Federal Reserve G.5A) ≈ $69.0 million.
  • "More than two thousand dollars for every square foot": about 161,100 yuan per square meter of permitted floor area (Feb. 2025, reported) at the 2025 average rate of 7.1875 yuan per dollar (Federal Reserve G.5A) ≈ $22,400 per square meter ≈ $2,080 per square foot. This is the price of the land per unit of floor space that may be built on it, not the price of finished homes.
  • "Roughly the length of a football field": the reported route out was about 48 meters west, then 46 meters north (≈ 94 m); an American football field is 100 yards (≈ 91 m).
  • "More than twice as many legs": 432 walking units under Huayanli (2025) against 198 under the school (2020).
  • "Nearly eleven hundred homes": 1,090 residential property certificates signed by January 22, 2019 (97.15 percent). A certificate usually covers one home; it is not necessarily one household.
  • "Well over a year": the buildings were walked out from the fall of 2023; Huayanli's return began on May 19, 2025.
  • Rounded on screen and in narration: 66.46 m ("about sixty-six meters"), 3.38 m ("more than three"), 61.7 m ("about sixty-two meters").

Worth knowing

  • We could not confirm which protection tier Huayanli is in. City notices call the buildings walked out of Zhangyuan's east side protected heritage buildings (文保建筑). The fines quoted in the video (one to three times the rebuilding cost for an unapproved move, three to five times for an unapproved demolition) apply to Shanghai's top tier, 'outstanding historic buildings' (Shanghai regulations, Articles 45 and 49, text in force since Dec. 28, 2023).
  • "Keep it on its original site": the national principle is set out in the explanation of the 2024 revision of China's Cultural Relics Protection Law (in force March 1, 2025): protection on the original site first; relocation or demolition only where truly necessary, with approval. For Huayanli, the Jing'an district government wrote that the return was meant to keep 'every brick' protected on its original site.
  • No published cost: we found no official figure for the cost of walking Huayanli (2023-2025), the school (2020) or the concert hall (2003). Figures circulating online could not be traced to an official source.
  • "Walking robots" is the contractor's and the city's name for the units; each is a pair of hydraulic jacks (one lifts, one pushes). The two-group stepping method shown is described in the contractor's patent CN110158990A.
  • The route out (48 m west, then 46 m north) comes from a single secondary source and is marked 'reported' on screen. The return followed the same route (Shanghai government, 2024).
  • Twenty-five buildings: the developer's count covers buildings walked away and back as well as buildings lifted where they stood.
  • "Virtual transfer": switching among Lines 2, 12 and 13 at Nanjing West Road has been described as a 'virtual transfer'; the new basement's stated core purpose is a transfer inside the station. We found no opening date for it.
  • The 2003 concert hall was pushed along rails, not walked. The 2020 school was walked on 198 units and set down on the roof of a new basement; it did not return.
  • Chinese quotations on this page are given in the original; English wording in the video is our translation.
