Why pour drinking water into the desert?

Out in the dunes of Abu Dhabi, it goes under the sand, into a buried bed of gravel.

For over two years, that water poured in.

  • The National, Jan 15, 2018 (joint EAD/ADWEA/Transco statement), 2018
    at a rate of 7 million imperial gallons per day over 27 months

Twenty-six billion liters.

  • The National, 2018 (via WAM, Sheikh Hazza's visit), 2018
    more than 26 million cubic metre (m3) of water (equivalent to 5.6 billion imperial gallons)

So why does the UAE pour its drinking water into the desert? It wasn't thrown away. Abu Dhabi, the UAE's capital emirate, means to drink it, on the day the sea lets the city down.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    harmful algae blooms and oil spills, energy supply and fear of war or terrorism
  • The National, Jan 15, 2018 (joint EAD/ADWEA/Transco statement), 2018
    We need strategic reserves in the event of one or more [desalination] stations being out of service

But the place they chose came with a catch. That sand already held water of its own, and that water isn't safe to drink.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    Cr-tot 52 87 115 129 123

So they had to pour clean water into the natural groundwater, and get clean water back.

Once it reaches the dunes, no pump pushes it into the ground.

Two days of water

In 2008, a red tide spread through the Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. It lasted into the next year.

  • Richlen, Morton, Jamali, Rajan, Anderson, Harmful Algae 9(2):163-172, 2010, 2010

Two thousand tanks

The obvious alternative was tanks.

  • Khaleej Times, 2015 (interview with EAD water resources adviser M. Dawoud), 2015 partly confirmed
    we would need to build 2,000 reservoirs

The Environment Agency's water adviser, Mohamed Dawoud, put a number on it. The same reserve would need two thousand of them, he said.

  • Khaleej Times, 2015 (interview with EAD water resources adviser M. Dawoud), 2015 partly confirmed
    we would need to build 2,000 reservoirs

But the count wasn't the worst part. Water standing in an open reservoir doesn't keep. He gave it five to seven days before it needs changing.

  • Khaleej Times, 2015 (interview with EAD water resources adviser M. Dawoud), 2015 partly confirmed
    we would need to build 2,000 reservoirs

A reserve has to wait for years. So the agency looked down instead. Underground, water is closed off from the air. But under Abu Dhabi, most of the ground holds salt water. Only three percent of its groundwater is fresh.

  • EAD, 'Groundwater Feature', Feb 2021, 2021
    79% of reserves have become highly saline, 18% are brackish, while only 3% can be considered fresh groundwater

The agency's surveys found only a few fresh pockets left. One lies under the dunes north of Liwa.

The water that was already there

But the ground wasn't empty. Follow the water down. It leaves the dry sand and reaches an aquifer: sand and soft rock with water in the gaps.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    14 (sub) horizontal layers in between ground surface and 40 m below sea level

The water at the top is fairly fresh. But it gets saltier with every layer.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    shallow 'fresh' groundwater on top of deep seated brackish to saline paleowater

At the bottom lies ancient water, with thirteen times the salt allowed in drinking water.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    TDS mg/L 682 ... 13,288 ... 1000

Salt is one problem. Chromium is the one that sneaks up on you.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    Cr-tot 52 87 115 129 123

Even in the top layer, the freshest one, the natural water carries chromium just over the drinking limit.

    Cr-tot 52 87 115 129 123

Deeper down, it's more than double.

But a reserve isn't pumped soon. It waits. So they ran the model again, with a ten-year wait.

In the model, over ten years, the bubble drifts. Even near the crest, the water table has a slight slope, and the whole mass slides sideways, away from the wells built to catch it.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017

Now only sixty percent comes back clean. After that, sodium, arsenic, chromium and fluoride start to cross their limits.

Ninety days, for how many?

The agency's chief, Razan Al Mubarak, called it the largest reserve of desalinated water ever stored in a natural aquifer.

  • Khaleej Times, Jan 15, 2018 (ADWEA announcement), 2018
    the largest reserve of high-quality desalinated water to be stored in a natural aquifer anywhere in the world

The claim is specific: desalinated water. Las Vegas has banked about seventeen times as much underground, but that's river water.

  • Las Vegas Valley Water District, 'Where your water comes from', 2026
    stored more than 360,000 acre-feet of water

And Dubai has been building a reserve designed to be slightly bigger.

  • Dubai Media Office, DEWA release, Mar 13, 2022, 2022 partly confirmed
    The cost of this innovative integrated model is less than the cost of traditional reservoirs

Why the pipe was never meant to close

The reserve was unveiled in January 2018.

  • IWMI GRIPP case profile (author M. A. Dawoud, EAD), 2020, 2020
    The full project was completed in December 2017

We found no public record that it has ever been called on. But the coast it backs up is changing. By 2031, Abu Dhabi's water utility expects membrane plants to cover ninety-two percent of the drinking water it supplies.

  • EWEC, Statement of Future Capacity Requirements 2024-2037, summary (Zawya), 2024, 2024
    bringing the total RO capacity to over 3.5 million cubic metres per day by 2031, which will serve 92 per cent

That's the kind that shut down at Fujairah, while the heat-driven plant ran on.

  • UNESCO-IOC Manuals and Guides 78 (Anderson, Boerlage, Dixon, eds.), 2017, 2017
    the SWRO desalination plant had to be shut down for more than one week

So why does the UAE pour its drinking water into the desert? Because in 2010, a city that drank the sea could store two days of it, and the ground could hold far more than tanks, for far longer.

  • The National, C. Stanton, Sept 23, 2010, 2010
    a system that today can store only two days' supply
  • Khaleej Times, 2015 (interview with EAD water resources adviser M. Dawoud), 2015 partly confirmed
    we would need to build 2,000 reservoirs

But the ground was never a tank. The water in it mixes, and it drifts.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    the recovery efficiency will drop from ~85 to 60%

So by design, the pipe in the dunes was never meant to close.

  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    The injection rate was raised by 20%, and during storage 568 m3/d will be infiltrated continuously
  • P. J. Stuyfzand, J. Smidt, K. G. Zuurbier, N. Hartog, M. A. Dawoud (EAD), Water 9(3):177, 2017, 2017
    This prediction has contributed to the implementation of some adaptations

It isn't a tank you fill once. It's a bubble you have to keep feeding.

Our calculations

  • "Twenty-six billion liters": 26 million cubic meters of desalinated water infiltrated over about 27 months (1 m³ = 1,000 liters).
  • "Ninety days" is the official design figure: one million people at 180 liters per person per day. That is roughly 4 to 6 percent of the emirate's normal water use, not a supply for everyone.
  • "About two thirds" and "day 80": from the 2017 model in Stuyfzand et al. Recovery of about 85 percent right after filling falls to about 60 percent after a ten-year wait, so 90 days of planned output shrinks to roughly 80. This is a model result, not a measurement.
  • "Seventeen times": the Las Vegas Valley water bank (about 360,000 acre-feet, about 444 million m³) against 26 million m³. Las Vegas banks river water, not desalinated water.

Worth knowing

  • The exact location of the site has not been published; maps in the video are schematic.
  • Filling ran by gravity through three buried gravel basins; the 315 wells only pump water back out. A small continuous feed keeps the stored water from drifting, by design; current operating data is not public.
  • The figures about operation (well counts in use, automatic switchover to spares) come from official statements, not independent measurement.
  • This channel's scripts are researched and drafted with AI tools, and the narration is a synthetic voice.
