  • City of Clewiston website, 2026 partly confirmed

In 1931, a new owner bought a failed sugar company here. That was the start of U.S. Sugar.

  • U.S. Sugar Corporation, 'About' page and BusinessWire (May 8, 2026), 2026

  • USDA ERS, Sugar and Sweeteners Outlook SSS-M-458 (16.10.2026), Tablo 4, 2026

  • USDA ERS, 'World, U.S., and Mexican sugar and corn sweetener prices' tablosu, 2026

So how does the American price stay that high? A federal sugar program stands behind it.

  • U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-24-106144 (31.10.2023), 2023

But a higher price is paid by somebody.

  • U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-24-106144 (31.10.2023), 2023

  • U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-24-106144 (31.10.2023), 2023

So the studies find a net loss to the economy, about a billion dollars a year.

  • U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-24-106144 (31.10.2023), 2023

So part of the bill for American sugar gets paid at the checkout, in every state.

7 of 7 · Florida's summit is 345 feet. So what are its hills made of?

Rockstar puts a mountain at its northern edge. Mount Kalaga, "a national landmark up against the state's northern border."

  • Rockstar Games, Mount Kalaga region text, 2026
    A national landmark up against the state’s northern border

The real Florida has a high point up against its northern border, too. It's called Britton Hill, and the Alabama line is less than a mile away.

  • U.S. Geological Survey, 'Elevations and Distances in the United States', 2026
    Florida Britton Hill Walton 345
  • USGS GNIS (via MyTopo, GNIS ID 1851954) and Geonomics calculation, 2026 partly confirmed
    Highest elevation in Florida at 345 feet.

But it's no mountain. The summit is three hundred forty-five feet above sea level, the lowest high point of any state.

  • U.S. Geological Survey, 'Elevations and Distances in the United States', 2026
    Florida Britton Hill Walton 345

So instead of a peak, Florida marks it with a stone monument in a small park.

  • highpoint.guide; Wikipedia (monument) partly confirmed

So where are Florida's steep hills? Some of them were built by people, out of waste.

  • U.S. EPA, 'TENORM: Fertilizer and Fertilizer Production Wastes', 2026
    5.2 tons of phosphogypsum is produced for every ton of phosphoric acid

In the spring of 2021, on Tampa Bay, one of them was at risk of collapse. It's called Piney Point, and on top of it sat a pond of wastewater.

  • Associated Press and WUSF (215 million gallons, $100 million); FDEP news release, Apr. 26, 2021, 2021 partly confirmed

So more than two hundred million gallons of that water went into Tampa Bay, according to news reports at the time.

  • Associated Press and WUSF (215 million gallons, $100 million); FDEP news release, Apr. 26, 2021, 2021 partly confirmed

So what is a hill like that? It's what fertilizer leaves behind. Phosphate rock is mined here, and across the country, almost all of it is turned into an acid for fertilizer.

  • U.S. Geological Survey, Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026, Phosphate Rock, 2026
    More than 95% of the phosphate rock mined in the United States was used to manufacture wet-process phosphoric acid

But each ton of that acid leaves about five tons of a chalky waste called phosphogypsum.

  • U.S. EPA, 'TENORM: Fertilizer and Fertilizer Production Wastes', 2026
    5.2 tons of phosphogypsum is produced for every ton of phosphoric acid

So it gets stacked, up to about two hundred feet above the flat ground. The state counts twenty-five of these stacks.

  • Florida Department of Environmental Protection, GYPSUMSTACKS open data layer (ArcGIS REST), 2026
  • U.S. EPA, 'TENORM: Fertilizer and Fertilizer Production Wastes', 2026
    5.2 tons of phosphogypsum is produced for every ton of phosphoric acid

The rock is worth nearly two billion dollars a year across the country. But the stacks stay behind, and closing one costs money.

Our calculations

  • "More than seven and a half billion dollars before tax" and "about a hundred million" in income tax: Carnival (fiscal year to Nov. 30, 2025) $2.772 billion pre-tax profit and $12 million income tax expense; Royal Caribbean (2025) $4.373 billion and $82 million; Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings (2025) $428.7 million and $5.5 million. Total: $7.574 billion before tax and $99.5 million in income tax expense, worldwide (SEC 10-K filings).
  • "A little more than a penny on the dollar": $99.5 million ÷ $7.574 billion ≈ 1.3%. Income tax expense only; port fees, payroll taxes and passenger taxes are not in this number, and the companies' fiscal years end on different dates.
  • "About one in five pounds of American sugar": Florida cane sugar 1.957 million short tons, raw value, out of a U.S. total of 9.28 million (USDA ERS forecast for 2025/26) ≈ 21%.
  • "Raw sugar costs about half what it costs in America": world raw sugar (ICE No. 11) 20.22 cents per pound against U.S. raw sugar (No. 16) 41.66 cents in October 2026 (USDA ERS) ≈ 0.49. The 2025 averages give a similar ratio (16.97 vs 35.97).
  • "The Alabama line is less than a mile away": Britton Hill sits at 30.9887°N (USGS GNIS); the Florida-Alabama line there runs along 31°N. 0.0113° × 111 km ≈ 1.25 km ≈ 0.8 miles.

Worth knowing

  • Rockstar Games has not said which real places its map is based on. The comparisons in this video (Leonida Keys and the Florida Keys, Grassrivers and the Everglades, Ambrosia and Clewiston, Port Gellhorn and the Forgotten Coast, Mount Kalaga and Britton Hill) are fans' and ours, based on Rockstar's own descriptions on its website.
  • No footage, screenshots, artwork or logos from GTA 6 are used in this video. Fictional place names appear only on our own maps.
  • The cruise companies did not pay "no tax". The figure here is the income tax expense each reports worldwide. Under Section 883 of the U.S. tax code, qualifying foreign shipping companies' income from operating ships is exempt from U.S. federal income tax; Carnival says this covers substantially all of its shipping income, Royal Caribbean says most of it.
  • The exact 1928 death toll can't be known. "More than two thousand five hundred" follows NOAA's National Weather Service.
  • The Tyndall rebuild figure is the Army Corps of Engineers' estimate as of June 2026 and covers the program, not a final cost.
  • Piney Point: "more than two hundred million gallons" follows news reports from April 2021 (AP, WUSF: about 215 million gallons). We could not find the figure on the state environmental agency's own pages.
  • Port Miami's passenger count and Port Canaveral's slightly higher count are each port's own figure for its own fiscal year.
