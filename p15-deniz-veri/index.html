What did Microsoft find?

Start with Microsoft's test. After a short trial off California, Microsoft sank a sealed cylinder the size of a shipping container off Orkney, in northern Scotland. That was 2018.

  • Microsoft Research, Project Natick Phase 1 page; Microsoft news, Sept. 14, 2020, 2015
    deployed the Leona Philpot system off the coast of California in 2015
  • Microsoft Research, Project Natick "Facts and figures", 2018-2020
    12 racks containing 864 standard Microsoft datacenter servers with FPGA acceleration and 27.6 petabytes of disk
  • Microsoft Research, Project Natick Phase 2 timeline and home page, 2020
    After more than two years on the seafloor, Natick Northern Isles was retrieved for analysis.

Inside were 864 servers, sealed in dry nitrogen instead of air.

  • Microsoft Research, Project Natick "Facts and figures", 2018-2020
    12 racks containing 864 standard Microsoft datacenter servers with FPGA acceleration and 27.6 petabytes of disk

It sat about thirty-six meters down. That's almost the same depth as the Chinese module.

  • People's Daily (Renmin Ribao), Jan. 16, 2024, 2023
    重达1300吨的“海底数据舱”稳稳沉入35米深的海底
  • Microsoft news (John Roach), Sept. 14, 2020; Microsoft Research, Project Natick home page, 2020
    Our failure rate in the water is one-eighth of what we see on land.

After more than two years, Microsoft pulled it up and counted what had broken.

  • Microsoft Research, Project Natick Phase 2 timeline and home page, 2020
    After more than two years on the seafloor, Natick Northern Isles was retrieved for analysis.

By Microsoft's own count, the servers underwater failed at one-eighth the rate of the same kind of servers on land.

  • Microsoft news (John Roach), Sept. 14, 2020; Microsoft Research, Project Natick home page, 2020
    Our failure rate in the water is one-eighth of what we see on land.

Microsoft's guess at why: nitrogen corrodes less than oxygen, and nobody walks in and bumps things.

  • Microsoft news (John Roach), Sept. 14, 2020; Microsoft Research, Project Natick home page, 2020

If China is moving data centers west, why the sea?

Here's China's problem, in the government's own words. Most of its data centers sit in the east, near the big coastal cities, where land and energy keep getting tighter.

  • China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), high-tech department Q&A, Feb. 17, 2022, 2022
    由于土地、能源等资源日趋紧张，在东部大规模发展数据中心难以为继

In 2022, the top planning agency called building them there on a large scale hard to sustain.

  • China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), high-tech department Q&A, Feb. 17, 2022, 2022
    由于土地、能源等资源日趋紧张，在东部大规模发展数据中心难以为继

Its answer was a national plan called East Data, West Computing. It drew eight national computing hubs, and pointed the arrows west, toward regions rich in renewable energy.

  • China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), high-tech department Q&A, Feb. 17, 2022, 2022
    由于土地、能源等资源日趋紧张，在东部大规模发展数据中心难以为继
  • NDRC high-tech department, Mar. 21, 2022, 2022
    同意在京津冀、长三角、粤港澳大湾区、成渝、内蒙古、贵州、甘肃、宁夏等8地启动建设国家算力枢纽节点

But you can't move everything. Some jobs need an answer in a blink: an AI replying to you, a financial trade, a factory line. The plan itself lets that kind of work stay in the east.

  • China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), high-tech department Q&A, Feb. 17, 2022, 2022
    由于土地、能源等资源日趋紧张，在东部大规模发展数据中心难以为继

So the east still needs computing close by. How tight does that get? Look at Shanghai. That same year, Shanghai put it in writing: any growth in its data centers' energy use would be strictly held to a quota set by the city government.

  • Shanghai Municipal Commission of Economy and Informatization et al., Hujingxinji [2022] No. 306, June 24, 2022, 2022
    按照市政府下达的“十四五”能耗指标严格控制数据中心能耗净增量

Now say you want more servers near Shanghai. You can't just buy a field, because land is tight. You can't just plug in more, because of the quota. And the work can't all go west.

  • China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), high-tech department Q&A, Feb. 17, 2022, 2022
    由于土地、能源等资源日趋紧张，在东部大规模发展数据中心难以为继
  • Shanghai Municipal Commission of Economy and Informatization et al., Hujingxinji [2022] No. 306, June 24, 2022, 2022
    按照市政府下达的“十四五”能耗指标严格控制数据中心能耗净增量

One company wrote that squeeze into its annual report. It builds undersea data centers, and it says they resolve a contradiction on the rich coast: surging demand for computing, and tightening energy quotas.

  • Beijing Highlander, 2025 annual report, 2026
    有效破解了沿海发达地区“算力需求激增”与“能耗指标收紧”的双重矛盾

How do you cool servers with seawater?

So what does the sea actually give you? Two things. First, room. By the project's own figures, the Shanghai site takes about a tenth of the land an equal data center would need on shore.

  • CCTV via CLS, May 15, 2026 (project lead Chen Xiyi), 2026
    一年淡水消耗量达4万吨

Think of the sea as a vacant lot, right next to downtown. Second, cooling. On land, getting rid of heat is a big part of the bill. By our math from the national average, about a third of a Chinese data center's power goes to things other than computing, like cooling.

  • China Academy of Information and Communications Technology (CAICT), green computing report, 2024, 2023
    平均电能利用效率（PUE）为 1.48

Underwater, the design is simple enough to draw. Pumps pull in seawater and push it through pipes to radiators on the back of each server rack.

  • Beijing Highlander, filing 2021-006 (Jan. 11, 2021), 2021
    冷却系统利用管道将海水直接通过服务器机架背面的散热器，然后排回大海

The water takes the heat, and goes back to the sea.

  • Beijing Highlander, filing 2021-006 (Jan. 11, 2021), 2021
    冷却系统利用管道将海水直接通过服务器机架背面的散热器，然后排回大海

Inside the shell there's inert gas instead of air. No oxygen, no dust, no people.

  • CCTV (cctv.com), via Guangming Online, Nov. 24, 2023, 2023
    商用海底数据中心采用重力热管原理，利用海水作为自然冷源
  • Beijing Highlander, filing 2021-006 (Jan. 11, 2021), 2021
    冷却系统利用管道将海水直接通过服务器机架背面的散热器，然后排回大海

That also means no one inside to swap a failed part. The company says none has been needed so far. It hasn't said what happens when one is.

  • Beijing Highlander, 2023 annual report; Xinhua, June 11, 2025 (shanghai.gov.cn), 2024
    总体规划建设智能算力超过 2000PFlops（FP16）

By the company's reports, underwater that overhead drops to about a tenth.

  • Beijing Highlander, 2023, 2024 and 2025 annual reports, 2023-2025
    PUE 保持 1.1 左右的全球领先水平

How does a tropical sea cool anything?

Hainan is a tropical island at China's southern edge. It's hot, and fresh water is scarce, which makes a data center on land expensive to cool.

  • People's Daily, July 11, 2023, 2023
    清水湾海域又处于琼东上升流范围，近岸海水温度低于24.5摄氏度

So why sink one here? Because of a current. Off eastern Hainan, from spring into early fall, monsoon winds push the surface water away, and colder water rises from below to replace it.

  • Zhu et al., Frontiers in Marine Science 10:1054669 (2023), satellite sea surface temperature 2003-2021, 2023 partly confirmed

Scientists call that upwelling. The company says the water near shore in this bay stays below about twenty-four and a half degrees Celsius.

  • People's Daily, July 11, 2023, 2023
    清水湾海域又处于琼东上升流范围，近岸海水温度低于24.5摄氏度

Is that cool enough? In an early test, the company found that seawater under twenty-five degrees kept the inside of a prototype cool enough for its servers.

  • Beijing Highlander, filing 2021-006, 2021
    在海水温度低于25℃时，可维持舱体环境温度低于35℃

So it works on paper, and it rests on the company's own numbers. We couldn't find independent temperature readings for the site. The first module went in at the end of 2022, as rented server space. The big one from our opening followed in November 2023. A module built for AI work joined them in 2025.

What could go wrong?

Start with sound. Researchers showed that sound played underwater can stall a submerged data center's drives.

  • Sheldon et al., IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy 2024 (arXiv:2404.11815), 2024
    an attacker can reduce fault-tolerant RAID 5 storage system throughput by 17% up to 100%

That was in a lab and a lake. We found no attack on a real site.

  • Sheldon et al., IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy 2024 (arXiv:2404.11815), 2024
    an attacker can reduce fault-tolerant RAID 5 storage system throughput by 17% up to 100%

Then heat. A report on the Shanghai project's own assessment says the water leaves about five degrees Celsius warmer than it came in. The assessment found no significant effect on the sea.

  • Dialogue Earth (Yuan Ye), Oct. 20, 2026, 2026 partly confirmed
    no significant effect on the marine environment

Outside scientists quoted in that report worry about summer heat waves. We couldn't find any independent measurements.

  • Dialogue Earth (Yuan Ye), Oct. 20, 2026, 2026 partly confirmed
    no significant effect on the marine environment

Then politics. Beijing Highlander, the listed company behind the project, has been on a U.S. export blacklist since 2022.

  • U.S. Federal Register, 87 FR 38920 (doc. 2022-14069), Bureau of Industry and Security, June 30, 2022, 2022
    for acquiring and attempting to acquire U.S.-origin items in support of military applications

The stated reason: acquiring, or trying to acquire, U.S.-origin items in support of military applications. HiCloud isn't named on that list.

So why didn't China give up?

Microsoft once made its case in a single line. Half of us live near the coast, so why doesn't our data?

  • Microsoft Research, Project Natick "Facts and figures", 2018-2020
    12 racks containing 864 standard Microsoft datacenter servers with FPGA acceleration and 27.6 petabytes of disk

The company building China's versions has put its reason in writing. More demand for computing, and tighter energy quotas, in the same coastal cities.

  • Beijing Highlander, 2025 annual report, 2026
    有效破解了沿海发达地区“算力需求激增”与“能耗指标收紧”的双重矛盾

Here's our guess, and it is a guess, since Microsoft never said why: Microsoft had other places to grow. Shanghai's limit is written into a city rule.

  • DatacenterDynamics, June 2024; heise online, June 25, 2024, 2024
    I'm not building subsea data centers anywhere in the world
  • Shanghai Municipal Commission of Economy and Informatization et al., Hujingxinji [2022] No. 306, June 24, 2022, 2022
    按照市政府下达的“十四五”能耗指标严格控制数据中心能耗净增量

That doesn't make the sea a good business. So far, the company's own books say it hasn't paid off as planned.

  • Beijing Highlander, 2025 annual report (cninfo, Apr. 22, 2026), 2025
    “海底数据中心一期项目”因部分客户正由传统机柜租赁向定制化、模块化部署模式转变
  • Beijing Highlander, 2023 and 2024 annual reports (financial data of major affiliates), 2023-2024 partly confirmed

Our calculations

  • "Roughly thirty million dollars, by our conversion": 215.1 million yuan (Hainan phase one, Beijing Highlander 2025 annual report) ÷ 7.1957 yuan per dollar (U.S. Federal Reserve, 2024 annual average; the money was spent in 2024) ≈ $29.9 million.
  • "About seven percent of that": returns of 14.57 million yuan reported for the project from its ready date in August 2024 to the end of 2025 (about 17 months) ÷ 215.1 million yuan invested ≈ 6.8%. The annual report does not define "returns"; we read it as the project's reported benefit, not profit.
  • "About a third of a Chinese data center's power goes to things other than computing": China's average power usage effectiveness (PUE) in 2023 was 1.48 (CAICT). Everything above 1.0 is overhead such as cooling: 0.48 ÷ 1.48 ≈ 32%.
  • "Underwater that overhead drops to about a tenth": the company reports a PUE of about 1.1 for the Hainan modules (2023-2025 annual reports, citing CAICT and China Telecom tests): 0.1 ÷ 1.1 ≈ 9%. This is the company's figure; we found no independent measurement.
  • "About a tenth of the land": the Shanghai project gives 200 m² for the undersea site against 2,000 m² for an equal data center on land (project figures, CCTV, May 2026).
  • "About twenty-three percent": the official saving for the Shanghai project is 22.8% less energy than a comparable data center on land (Lin-gang Special Area, Oct. 2025). The comparison baseline was not published.
  • "About two hundred twenty million dollars": 1.6 billion yuan of planned first investment across two phases (Xinhua, June 2025), reported by Xinhua as about $222.7 million.
  • "About ten times that size": phase one is 2.3 MW; the two-phase plan is 24 MW (Xinhua, June 2025): 24 ÷ 2.3 ≈ 10.4.
  • "Less than one five-hundredth of the electricity China's data centers use" and "< 0.2%" (on screen): 24 MW running at full load all year ≈ 0.21 TWh. China's data centers used about 150 TWh in 2023 (CAICT): 0.21 ÷ 150 ≈ 0.14%, below 1/500 (0.2%). Rough: real load is lower than full load, and national use has grown since 2023.
  • Unit conversions on screen: 117 ft ≈ 36 m (Natick; Microsoft gives 117 feet); 35 m ≈ 115 ft (Hainan); 10 m ≈ 33 ft; 24.5 °C ≈ 76 °F; 15 °C ≈ 59 °F; a 5 °C rise ≈ 9 °F.

Worth knowing

  • Beijing Highlander has not published repair or maintenance procedures for the sealed modules. It says none has been needed so far (2023 annual report; Xinhua, June 2025).
  • We found no independent measurements of the water temperature at the Hainan site or of the warm-water discharge in Shanghai. The temperatures in this video are the company's figures or come from the project's own assessment as reported by Dialogue Earth.
  • Microsoft has not said why it stopped Project Natick. In June 2024 a Microsoft executive told DatacenterDynamics the company was not building subsea data centers anywhere and that the project "worked"; Microsoft Research describes Natick as a research platform. "Microsoft had other places to grow" is our guess, not Microsoft's statement.
  • Plans: a 2021 local report said about 100 modules were planned in Hainan (IT Home, citing the Sanya investment promotion bureau). We could confirm three modules reported in 2023 and an AI module in 2025; the exact count today was not confirmed.
  • The U.S. Entity List entry (June 2022) names Beijing Highlander Digital Technology and three related companies. HiCloud, the affiliate that builds and runs the modules, is not named.
  • Chinese-language quotes on screen (NDRC, Shanghai data center rules, Beijing Highlander annual report) are our translations.
