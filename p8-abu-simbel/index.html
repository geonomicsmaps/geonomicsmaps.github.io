How Egypt Moved Abu Simbel Before the Nile Drowned It

Geonomics. Every narrated claim with its source and a short quote in the original language. Errors? Say so in the video's comments; corrections are pinned.

A temple that moved 65 meters up

This is Abu Simbel. Ramesses the Second had it carved into a cliff.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978); UNESCO WHC, "Working Together: Abu Simbel" (World Heritage n°103, Kas 2022), 1978
    The façade is dominated by four colossal statues of the King, each about 20 m. high ... The innermost room, the Bark Sanctuary, is located about 60 m. …

But this isn't where it was carved. The original cliff is now under the lake.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The horizontal transfer of the two temples was 208 metres, approximately to the north-west. Vertically, the Great Temple was lifted 65 metres and the Small Temple 67 metres.
  • L. Berg (VBB), Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS); secondary Wikipedia 'Abu Simbel temples', 1978 partly confirmed
    originally the temples were situated a few metres above the Nile waters ... high never to be reached by Lake Nasser

So how do you move a temple carved into a mountain, before the water gets to it?

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978); UNESCO WHC, "Working Together: Abu Simbel" (World Heritage n°103, Kas 2022), 1978
    The façade is dominated by four colossal statues of the King, each about 20 m. high ... The innermost room, the Bark Sanctuary, is located about 60 m. …
  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    they were to rise 8 metres already during 1964 and a further 5 metres the following year, that is up to SSL 133 ... fully dismantled during the …

The short answer: you cut it into more than a thousand pieces, and carry it uphill.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    the 1,036 blocks of stone, which weighed between seven and thirty tonnes each
  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The horizontal transfer of the two temples was 208 metres, approximately to the north-west. Vertically, the Great Temple was lifted 65 metres and the Small Temple 67 metres.

And when you get there, you build the mountain yourself. Around a giant concrete dome.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The dome over the Great Temple has a free span of about 60 m. and a height of 25 m. Carrying in all about 100,000 metric tons in …
  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The artificial hill around and above each temple, replacing the original cliff into which the temple had once been carved.

Problem 1: The water is coming

In 1960, Egypt began a giant dam at Aswan, built with Soviet help.

  • UNESCO Courier XXXIII 2/3 (Feb-Mar 1980), "Victory in Nubia", kronoloji kutusu, 1980
    9 January 1960 ... Work on the Aswan High Dam officially inaugurated
  • U.S. Department of State, FRUS 1958-1960 vol. XIII, doc. 256 (Cairo embassy telegram, 20 Jan 1960), 1960
    UAR agreement for Soviet assistance in construction both stages high dam ... USSR sole source foreign assistance for most important and spectacular UAR economic project

Behind it, a lake would grow about five hundred kilometers long, deep into Sudan. Everything in its path would go under.

  • UNESCO Courier XXXIII 2/3 (Feb-Mar 1980), "Victory in Nubia", s.15, 1980
    The water impounded behind the dam has created a 500-kilometre-long lake, the world's second largest man-made lake (after the Kariba Dam lake
  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt"; L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1980
    the waters of the Nile would be raised by a further sixty-two metres above sea level

For the people living along the river, that meant losing their homes.

  • M. Janmyr, "The Nubians of Egypt: a displaced population", Atlas of Contemporary Egypt (CNRS Éditions, 2023), 2023
    required the transfer of roughly 50,000 inhabitants in 1963-1964
  • A. and W. Kronenberg, "Nubische Werte und der Wiederaufbau von Wadi Halfa", Paideuma 30 (1984), 1984
    5. 1.1964 Beginn der Umsiedlung nach Khashim el Girba. September 1964 Wadi Haifa wird überschwemmt und zerstört.

In Egypt, the government moved roughly fifty thousand Nubians to new villages near Kom Ombo.

  • M. Janmyr, "The Nubians of Egypt: a displaced population", Atlas of Contemporary Egypt (CNRS Éditions, 2023), 2023
    required the transfer of roughly 50,000 inhabitants in 1963-1964

In Sudan, the town of Wadi Halfa was emptied too.

  • A. and W. Kronenberg, "Nubische Werte und der Wiederaufbau von Wadi Halfa", Paideuma 30 (1984), 1984
    5. 1.1964 Beginn der Umsiedlung nach Khashim el Girba. September 1964 Wadi Haifa wird überschwemmt und zerstört.

Those villages weren't saved. They're under the lake now.

  • A. and W. Kronenberg, "Nubische Werte und der Wiederaufbau von Wadi Halfa", Paideuma 30 (1984), 1984
    5. 1.1964 Beginn der Umsiedlung nach Khashim el Girba. September 1964 Wadi Haifa wird überschwemmt und zerstört.
  • L. Berg (VBB), Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS); secondary Wikipedia 'Abu Simbel temples', 1978 partly confirmed
    originally the temples were situated a few metres above the Nile waters ... high never to be reached by Lake Nasser

For the temple, it meant drowning. The river would rise about sixty meters, far above the statues.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt"; L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1980
    the waters of the Nile would be raised by a further sixty-two metres above sea level
  • L. Berg (VBB), Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS); secondary Wikipedia 'Abu Simbel temples', 1978 partly confirmed
    originally the temples were situated a few metres above the Nile waters ... high never to be reached by Lake Nasser

And sandstone doesn't last underwater. It loses its strength.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    the sandstone in the temple region suffers considerable losses in strength and durability when submerged

In 1964, the Nile was diverted, and the water behind the dam began to climb.

  • UNESCO Courier XXXIII 2/3 (Feb-Mar 1980), "Victory in Nubia", kronoloji kutusu, 1980
    14 May 1964 Diversion of Nile to feed the turbines of the High Dam; September 1964 The water begins to rise behind the High Dam

The engineers planned to have the temples out by the spring of 1966.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    they were to rise 8 metres already during 1964 and a further 5 metres the following year, that is up to SSL 133 ... fully dismantled during the …

So first, they bought time. They built a cofferdam: a temporary wall that keeps water out of a work site.

  • UNESCO Courier XVIII 11 (Nov 1965), L. A. Christophe, "Dismantling Abu Simbel", 1965
    Had this coffer-dam not raised its protective mass before the temples of Abu Simbel, they would have been submerged and damaged during the winter of 1964-65
  • Webuild, project page "Abu Simbel Temples Rescue", 2026 partly confirmed
    which measured 370 m in length and 25 m in height

Behind that wall, you could keep working in the dry while the lake rose on the other side.

  • UNESCO Courier XVIII 11 (Nov 1965), L. A. Christophe, "Dismantling Abu Simbel", 1965
    Had this coffer-dam not raised its protective mass before the temples of Abu Simbel, they would have been submerged and damaged during the winter of 1964-65

Without it, the temples would have been flooded by the winter of 1964 to 1965, long before the job was done.

  • UNESCO Courier XVIII 11 (Nov 1965), L. A. Christophe, "Dismantling Abu Simbel", 1965
    Had this coffer-dam not raised its protective mass before the temples of Abu Simbel, they would have been submerged and damaged during the winter of 1964-65

Problem 2: Five plans, one budget

In 1960, UNESCO asked the world for help.

  • UNESCO WHC, "Working Together: Abu Simbel" (World Heritage n°103, Kas 2022), 2022
    On 8 March 1960, the International Campaign to Save the Monuments of Nubia was launched at UNESCO. Director-General Vittorino Veronese stressed the monuments' universal value.

The world sent ideas. Some of them were wild.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    The first plan turned out to be financially onerous ($82 million), not to speak of the damage to the temples which would be caused by damp arising from …
  • L. Allais, "Integrities: The Salvage of Abu Simbel", Grey Room 50 (2013), 2013
    the most fantastic was a British proposal to leave the temples underwater ... Albert Caquot, the proposal was to "float" the temples to a new site in synchronicity …

One plan: wall the temple in behind its own dam, for eighty-two million dollars.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    The first plan turned out to be financially onerous ($82 million), not to speak of the damage to the temples which would be caused by damp arising from …

And water seeping up through the ground could still have damaged the stone.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    The first plan turned out to be financially onerous ($82 million), not to speak of the damage to the temples which would be caused by damp arising from …

Another: seal the whole temple in a giant concrete box and jack it up the cliff, one centimeter at a time.

  • L. Allais, "Integrities: The Salvage of Abu Simbel", Grey Room 50 (2013), 2013
    encased in a gigantic concrete box and lifted about sixty meters, one centimeter at a time, by a grid of hydraulic jacks

The wildest ideas? Leave the temples underwater, right where they were. Or float them up on the rising lake itself, and let the water do the lifting.

  • L. Allais, "Integrities: The Salvage of Abu Simbel", Grey Room 50 (2013), 2013
    the most fantastic was a British proposal to leave the temples underwater ... Albert Caquot, the proposal was to "float" the temples to a new site in synchronicity …

If you had to choose, which would you pick? The plan that won was the one experts had recoiled from: cut the temples into blocks and rebuild them higher up.

  • L. Allais, "Integrities: The Salvage of Abu Simbel", Grey Room 50 (2013), 2013 partly confirmed
    VBB's scheme was three times cheaper than the other proposals and was adopted in extremis in June 1963

It came from a Swedish firm, VBB, and it was by far the cheapest, at about thirty-six million dollars.

  • L. Allais, "Integrities: The Salvage of Abu Simbel", Grey Room 50 (2013), 2013 partly confirmed
    VBB's scheme was three times cheaper than the other proposals and was adopted in extremis in June 1963
  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    estimated to amount to approximately 36 million US dollars ... UNESCO would pay up to 20.5 million dollars ... The largest contributor, after the U.A.R., was the U.S.A., …

It was adopted at the last minute, in June 1963.

  • L. Allais, "Integrities: The Salvage of Abu Simbel", Grey Room 50 (2013), 2013 partly confirmed
    VBB's scheme was three times cheaper than the other proposals and was adopted in extremis in June 1963

In the end, the move cost about forty-two million dollars, more than the estimate.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    originally estimated at $36 million, eventually ran to $41.7 million (18.5 million Egyptian pounds)
  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    estimated to amount to approximately 36 million US dollars ... UNESCO would pay up to 20.5 million dollars ... The largest contributor, after the U.A.R., was the U.S.A., …

Egypt paid half. Dozens of countries paid the rest.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    A total of forty-eight nations helped to finance the project, with Egypt alone shouldering half the cost.
  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    originally estimated at $36 million, eventually ran to $41.7 million (18.5 million Egyptian pounds)

The biggest donor was the United States, at about twelve million dollars' worth.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978

Washington planned to pay in Egyptian pounds it already held, from selling farm surplus to Egypt.

Problem 3: How do you cut a statue?

Before the real cutting began, they buried the front of the temple.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    In November, 1964, a steel culvert through the sandfill in front of the Great Temple was erected, to form an access to the temple rooms.

The giant statues disappeared under a hill of sand, with a steel tunnel through it so workers could still get inside.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    In November, 1964, a steel culvert through the sandfill in front of the Great Temple was erected, to form an access to the temple rooms.

Then they took the mountain off the top. By November 1965, they had removed three hundred thousand tons of rock from above the Great Temple.

  • UNESCO Courier XVIII 11 (Nov 1965), L. A. Christophe, "Dismantling Abu Simbel", 1965
    300,000 tons of rock have been removed; the hill above the temple has been excavated.

Only a shell of rock behind the carvings, about a meter thick, was kept.

  • L. Berg (VBB), Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS), 1978
    generally between 60 and 120 cm ... about 80 cm

The rest of that mountain had to go.

  • UNESCO Courier XVIII 11 (Nov 1965), L. A. Christophe, "Dismantling Abu Simbel", 1965
    300,000 tons of rock have been removed; the hill above the temple has been excavated.

Now the cut. The rule was simple: no saw line could run through a face.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    no cuts could be permitted across the faces of statues

Every face had to come out in one piece.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    no cuts could be permitted across the faces of statues

On the carved surfaces, they cut by hand, with hand saws. No cut could be wider than eight millimeters, thinner than a pencil.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    Cutting in the visible temple surfaces, where the cuts had to be very thin (maximum 8 mm), was done practically exclusively by means of hand saws ... motor-driven …

From behind, where nobody would ever look, they used power saws.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    Cutting in the visible temple surfaces, where the cuts had to be very thin (maximum 8 mm), was done practically exclusively by means of hand saws ... motor-driven …

You get one try at each cut. There's no undo on a wall that has stood for more than three thousand years.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978); UNESCO WHC, "Working Together: Abu Simbel" (World Heritage n°103, Kas 2022), 1978
    The façade is dominated by four colossal statues of the King, each about 20 m. high ... The innermost room, the Bark Sanctuary, is located about 60 m. …

And because the sandstone was fragile, they injected it with resin before the saws went in.

  • L. Allais, "Integrities: The Salvage of Abu Simbel", Grey Room 50 (2013), 2013
    was injected three times: before the temples were cut, as the blocks were fastened to their moving anchors, and after the entire complex was reassembled

The work went to a team of six construction companies. They came from Germany, Egypt, France, Italy, and Sweden, and they worked as one crew.

  • UNESCO Courier XXXIII 2/3 (Feb-Mar 1980), "Victory in Nubia", s.8-9 kutusu, 1980
    Hochtief, Essen; Atlas, Cairo; Grands Travaux de Marseille, Paris; Impregilo, Milan; Santab and Skanska, Stockholm

Blocks from the front could weigh up to thirty tons. Blocks from inside, up to twenty.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    It was specified in the Contract that the weight of the blocks taken from the façades was not to exceed 30 tons, while the weight of the blocks …

By UNESCO's count, the two temples became a puzzle of one thousand and thirty-six pieces.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    the 1,036 blocks of stone, which weighed between seven and thirty tonnes each

Problem 4: Twenty tons in the air

So how do you pick up a block of carved stone without touching the carving?

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    vertical holes were rotary-drilled from the upper surface of the block down to about 0.2 m from the bottom surface ... No lifting devices of any kind were …

From the top. Workers drilled deep holes down into each block and fixed steel bars at the bottom of the holes, two to four per block.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    vertical holes were rotary-drilled from the upper surface of the block down to about 0.2 m from the bottom surface ... No lifting devices of any kind were …

The crane's hook was attached to the bars. No lifting gear ever touched the decorated surface.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    vertical holes were rotary-drilled from the upper surface of the block down to about 0.2 m from the bottom surface ... No lifting devices of any kind were …

One by one, the blocks went onto trailers and up to a storage yard.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    vertical holes were rotary-drilled from the upper surface of the block down to about 0.2 m from the bottom surface ... No lifting devices of any kind were …

Even the ceiling of the innermost room came out in three pieces, each up to twenty tons.

  • UNESCO Courier XVIII 11 (Nov 1965), L. A. Christophe, "Dismantling Abu Simbel", 1965
    in August 1965, the first ceiling, that of the sanctuary, was cut into three blocks, each weighing from 15 to 20 tons. These blocks were lifted by a …

Remember the plan? Out by the spring of 1966.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    they were to rise 8 metres already during 1964 and a further 5 metres the following year, that is up to SSL 133 ... fully dismantled during the …

The last blocks came out in February and March of 1966.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt"; UNESCO WHC, "Working Together: Abu Simbel" (World Heritage n°103, Kas 2022), 1980
    Work on transferring the two temples was completed in February/ March 1966 ... This operation was finished in September 1967 ... Work ended in September 1968, and the …

They finished on schedule. And in all that cutting and lifting, not a single block was lost. A few were damaged along the way, but all were repaired.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    not one temple block was lost during all the different operations

Problem 5: The missing mountain

The new site was sixty-five meters higher, set back from the water.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The horizontal transfer of the two temples was 208 metres, approximately to the north-west. Vertically, the Great Temple was lifted 65 metres and the Small Temple 67 metres.

And the temples were turned to face the same direction as before.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The horizontal transfer of the two temples was 208 metres, approximately to the north-west. Vertically, the Great Temple was lifted 65 metres and the Small Temple 67 metres.

They put the puzzle back together, piece by piece.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt"; UNESCO WHC, "Working Together: Abu Simbel" (World Heritage n°103, Kas 2022), 1980
    Work on transferring the two temples was completed in February/ March 1966 ... This operation was finished in September 1967 ... Work ended in September 1968, and the …

This is one of Ramesses' faces, being lifted back into place in 1967. It weighed about twenty tons.

  • Forskning & Framsteg 1967 no. 3 p.16 (caption of Per-Olow Anderson's photo), Wikimedia Commons File:Abusimbel.jpg, 1967 partly confirmed
    Ansiktet väger 19,6 ton och betongförstärkningen i dess baksida 4,58 ton. Mellan mungiporna är avståndet 108 cm.

The joints were filled with mortar made from crushed sandstone, so most of them almost vanish.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    After filling, the joints in the temple rooms, as well as those in the façade statues, were almost impossible to distinguish. Only in the upper, very smooth surface …

But there was one thing they couldn't bring with them: the mountain. The temple had been carved into a cliff, and that cliff was gone.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The artificial hill around and above each temple, replacing the original cliff into which the temple had once been carved.

So they built a new one. Over the Great Temple sits a reinforced concrete dome, about sixty meters across.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The dome over the Great Temple has a free span of about 60 m. and a height of 25 m. Carrying in all about 100,000 metric tons in …

The hill you see is piled on top of it: stone walls in the front, rock fill behind.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The artificial hill around and above each temple, replacing the original cliff into which the temple had once been carved.
  • L. Berg (VBB), 'The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples', Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS), 1978
    Rather than creating a poor imitation of Nature, definitely 'stylized' hills would be preferable ... With means starting to peter out, the uppermost parts of the cliff-sides were …

The dome carries roughly a hundred thousand tons.

  • L. Berg (VBB), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS, 1978), 1978
    The dome over the Great Temple has a free span of about 60 m. and a height of 25 m. Carrying in all about 100,000 metric tons in …

If you walk around the back, the hill doesn't look quite natural. That's on purpose. The engineers decided a clearly designed hill was better than a poor copy of nature.

  • L. Berg (VBB), 'The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples', Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS), 1978
    Rather than creating a poor imitation of Nature, definitely 'stylized' hills would be preferable ... With means starting to peter out, the uppermost parts of the cliff-sides were …

And when money ran short, the highest parts of the stone walls got a cheaper finish.

  • L. Berg (VBB), 'The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples', Monumentum 17 (ICOMOS), 1978

Four temples that left Nubia

Abu Simbel wasn't the only thing saved.

  • UNESCO WHC, "Working Together: Abu Simbel" (World Heritage n°103, Kas 2022), 2022
    In all, 22 monuments and complexes were saved by 40 technical missions from five continents ... Total cost came to US$80 million, half of which was donated by …

The Egyptian government thanked the countries that helped by giving them four temples from Nubia.

  • UNESCO Courier (1980), Shehata Adam Mohamed, "Victory in Nubia: Egypt", 1980
    Dendur was given to the United States, Dabod to Spain, Taffa to the Netherlands, and the chapels of Ellesiya to Italy.

You can still visit them. The Temple of Dendur, finished about two thousand years ago under the Roman emperor Augustus, went to New York.

  • The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Temple of Dendur (68.154), 2026

  • Ayuntamiento de Madrid, press note (19 Jul 2022), 2022

Our calculations

  • "About $40,000 per block" (on screen): $42 million ÷ 1,036 blocks ≈ $40,500 (UNESCO Courier 1980; our math, 1960s dollars, not inflation-adjusted).
  • Lake length: about 500 km (UNESCO) ≈ 310 miles; FAO gives 481 km for the full reservoir at the 180 m level, including the Sudanese part (Lake Nubia).
  • "Sixty-five meters higher": VBB/Berg 1978 (Great Temple 65 m; Small Temple 67 m); UNESCO's own pages give 64 m. 65 m ≈ 213 ft.
  • U.S. share: $12 million = 56% of the $21.47 million international fund for Abu Simbel (Allais 2013; VBB 1978).

Worth knowing

  • Block counts differ between sources (1,030 to 1,070); we use UNESCO's 1,036 (UNESCO Courier 1980). Weights: 7 to 30 tonnes each; façade blocks were capped at 30 t, room blocks at 20 t (VBB contract, Berg 1978).
  • A figure of "320,000 tonnes of blocks" circulates online. It cannot be the weight of the moved blocks (1,036 × at most 30 t ≈ 31,000 t). We use the documented 300,000 tons of rock removed from above the Great Temple by November 1965 (UNESCO Courier 1965).
  • Sun alignment: the current dates (around 22 February and 22 October) and "about 20 minutes" come from news reports (Ahram Online / AFP, October 2026), not a primary source. The claim that moving the temple shifted the date by one day is also from those reports. The "birthday and coronation" story has no ancient source that we could find.
  • The idea that cutting won partly because it could be paid in local currency is the argument of historian Lucia Allais (Grey Room, 2013). VBB states that more than 40% of the estimate still had to be paid in convertible currency. Kennedy's 1961 message proposed paying the U.S. contribution with U.S.-held Egyptian pounds from PL 480 sales; we did not find a document showing in which currency the $12 million was finally paid.
  • About 50,000 Egyptian Nubians were resettled near Kom Ombo in 1963-1964 (academic estimate). Wadi Halfa's residents were moved to Khashm el-Girba from January 1964. Village locations on the closing map are illustrative.
  • Today's cities of the temples given to the Netherlands (Taffeh) and Italy (Ellesiya) are not named in our primary sources; the map shows countries only.
